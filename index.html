Record to this device streams every raw sensor sample, the mic's level track, the steps and the clocks to this browser and saves a .krec file when you stop — any length, but the device has to come along (Chrome on Android; the screen stays on while recording). Record on the pod uses its 2 MB flash: about five minutes, no phone needed, download it here afterwards. Every start and stop flashes the ring white in a coded pattern: film it, and kine_label.html lines the video up with the recording. Stomp three times after the flashes, too — the mic and the video's sound both hear it.

The ring above is a preview of the program you selected, not a readback — the pod sends pixels to the strip, not to here. Brightness is capped at 150 by the firmware and capped again when the cell gets low: twelve RGBW pixels off the 3V3 pin can outdraw the IMU and the radio together.

Drift compares the last 16 steps against this runner's own first 40 — no population norms, no notion of correct form. It rises when contact time or duty factor climb, when step timing becomes erratic, or when the foot stops being carried as high.

Frontal-plane excursion is measured only while the foot is flat and discarded at toe-off, so it never contaminates the timing features. It is an experimental proxy, not a pronation measurement — nothing fires on it until it has been validated against motion capture.

Strike level is how loud the landing was, from the Sense board’s microphone. Landing harder is one of the first things a tiring runner does, and step timing barely shows it. Mic lead is the IMU’s contact time minus the strike’s click — a second, independent opinion on when the foot hit. On the ESKF firmware every landing is checked: mic-confirmed is the share the mic heard as this foot’s strike (the other foot, half a stride later, is quieter and does not count). A landing it did not hear is left out of the fatigue trend; on a surface it cannot hear (grass, a loud treadmill) it says so and gives no opinion. The pod turns audio into a level and a timestamp as it arrives; no audio is stored, and none is sent.
